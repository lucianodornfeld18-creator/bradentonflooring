Product Systems

Commercial LVT, carpet tile and rolled goods — matched to the duty cycle

Glue-down commercial LVT is the workhorse of Gulf Coast commercial interiors: 20, 22 or 28 mil wear layers over dimensionally stable cores, direct-bonded so point loads from desks, racks and rolling carts can't flex the joints. The mil number is the spec that matters — 20 mil suits standard office duty, while 28 mil belongs in retail aisles and healthcare corridors where traffic never lets up.

Carpet tile earns its keep through logistics: 24-inch modules install around occupied furniture, damaged squares swap out in minutes instead of triggering a full replacement, and mixed-pattern layouts hide soiling between cleanings. Rolled goods — heat-welded sheet vinyl and broadloom — remain the right call where seams are the enemy: clinical spaces needing monolithic, coved, washable surfaces, and executive areas where a seamless expanse reads more premium than any modular product. We install all three systems and will happily quote two side by side when the right answer isn't obvious.

Compliance Built In

Slip resistance, DCOF and ADA transitions

Wet-zone floors carry legal exposure, so we specify by test data rather than by adjectives. For level interior areas that get wet in service — kitchens, bars, restrooms, entries facing summer downpours — we select products with a published wet DCOF of 0.42 or higher under ANSI A326.3, and push beyond that minimum in back-of-house cooking zones. Those test reports stay in your project file, where your insurer and your inspector both like to find them. Transitions get the same rigor: the ADA allows a maximum 1/4-inch vertical change (1/2-inch if beveled), and every doorway, ramp and material change we install is detailed to those numbers so a wheelchair, a walker and a loaded handtruck all cross without a bump or a citation.

Protocol Spotlight

Slab Moisture & Flatness — all 11 checkpoints, commercial grade

Commercial slabs fail floors faster than residential ones: bigger pours, more cold joints, and grade-level moisture that never sleeps. That's why the largest category of our 47-Point Coastal Subfloor & Install Protocol — alongside Acoustic & HOA Compliance (8 points), Material Acclimation & Layout (9), Installation & Transitions (12) and Final Walkthrough & Care Plan (7) — is devoted entirely to concrete. Here is the full eleven, exactly as our commercial crews run it:

  1. Visual slab survey — cracks, joints, curl and previous patch history logged.
  2. In-situ RH probes — ASTM F2170 relative-humidity testing at code-required depth and density.
  3. Calcium-chloride tests — ASTM F1869 vapor-emission measurement where the adhesive spec calls for it.
  4. Surface pH check — alkalinity verified against adhesive tolerance before a drop is spread.
  5. Flatness mapping — 10-foot straightedge survey against the product's FF requirement, documented zone by zone.
  6. High-spot grinding — ridges and curled joints cut down, dust-controlled.
  7. Self-leveling placement — primed, pumped underlayment restoring plane in low areas.
  8. Crack & joint treatment — isolation or fill strategy assigned per movement type.
  9. Moisture mitigation decision — epoxy vapor-control systems deployed on grade when readings exceed product limits.
  10. Barrier & underlayment selection — matched to the readings and the finished floor, never generic.
  11. Adhesive compatibility sign-off — readings, mitigation and adhesive warranty conditions reconciled in writing before installation begins.

After the Install

Maintenance programs that protect the warranty

Commercial floor warranties are conditional on documented maintenance, and most premature failures we're called to assess trace back to the wrong cleaning chemistry, not the wrong product. Every turnover packet therefore includes a maintenance specification your janitorial vendor can execute: approved neutral cleaners and autoscrubber pad grades for LVT, hot-water extraction intervals for carpet tile, and entry-matting recommendations that intercept the shell grit Gulf Coast foot traffic drags in — the same abrasive that dulls wear layers fastest. Commercial Questions

Commercial flooring FAQs from Bradenton businesses

Can you install commercial flooring at night or on weekends?

Yes — a large share of our commercial work happens outside business hours precisely so yours don't stop. Retail and restaurant floors typically go in overnight between close and open, and office projects run over weekends with furniture lift-and-shift included. Night and weekend labor is priced into the bid up front, not added as a surprise change order.

Can you provide a certificate of insurance (COI) for our building?

Yes. We carry general liability and workers' compensation coverage and can issue a COI naming your property owner or management company as certificate holder, usually within one business day of the request. If your building requires specific endorsement language or coverage minimums, send the requirements with your bid request and the paperwork will be ready before mobilization.

How do you phase flooring in an office that stays occupied?

We break the plan into zones that follow your seating chart: one section of workstations relocates for 24 to 48 hours while its floor is replaced, then the crew leapfrogs to the next zone. Carpet tile and glue-down LVT suit this approach because sections go back into service almost immediately, and low-VOC adhesives keep occupied areas usable. You receive the zone map and calendar with the bid so department leads can plan around it.

What DCOF rating do restaurant and wet-area floors need?

The commonly enforced benchmark is a wet DCOF of 0.42 or higher (per the ANSI A326.3 test) for level interior floors expected to get wet — kitchens, bar service lines, restrooms and entry vestibules. Back-of-house kitchens often warrant more aggressive slip-resistant surfaces beyond the minimum. We specify products with published DCOF test data for each zone and keep those spec sheets in your project file for inspections and insurance audits.

What does commercial LVT cost installed in the Bradenton area?

Most 2026 commercial glue-down LVT projects in Manatee and Sarasota counties land between $4 and $9 per square foot installed depending on wear layer (20 to 28 mil), plank format and slab prep needs; carpet tile typically runs $3 to $7. Commercial work is bid per project because moisture mitigation, phasing and after-hours labor move the number — every bid itemizes those factors so you can compare apples to apples.

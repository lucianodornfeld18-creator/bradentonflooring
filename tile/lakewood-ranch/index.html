The Ranch Tile Brief

Big tile, bright bathrooms, brutal sun — the three jobs Ranch owners hire us for

Tile taste in Lakewood Ranch has moved decisively toward scale. The 12x12 beige squares that came with the early Country Club homes are being demoed by the truckload, replaced with 24x48 and even 48x48 porcelain panels that make a great room read like a single stone surface. It's a stunning look — and it's the least forgiving installation in residential flooring, because every extra inch of tile length amplifies whatever the slab beneath it gets wrong.

The second signature project is the master bath. Wild Blue and The Lake Club buyers arrive with saved photos of hotel-spa bathrooms: floor-to-ceiling porcelain walls, niche shelving lit from above, linear drains, glass with no curb to step over. Behind every one of those images is unglamorous engineering — recessed slabs, sloped mud beds, continuous waterproofing membranes flood-tested before tile — and it's exactly the part of the job you can't inspect after grout day, which is why ours gets photographed and documented as it goes.

Third: the lanai. Outdoor living is the whole point of a golf-course lot, and tiling it means designing for thermal shock, wind-driven rain and wet barefoot traffic. Exterior-rated porcelain, uncoupling membranes, movement joints in the field — the Installation & Transitions category of our 47-Point Coastal Subfloor & Install Protocol was practically written on Ranch lanais.

Lakewood Ranch Tile Questions

Answers for the projects Ranch owners are planning

Why does large-format tile in Lakewood Ranch require extra floor prep?

A 24x48 porcelain panel bridges four feet of floor with zero tolerance for dips. Industry standards tighten the flatness requirement for any tile with a side longer than 15 inches to 1/8 inch over 10 feet — roughly twice as strict as for small tile — and almost no Ranch slab meets that as-poured. We grind and self-level to the tighter spec first, because thin-set cannot fix what the substrate gets wrong, and hollow spots under big tile become cracks within a couple of seasons.

Can you tile our lanai and outdoor kitchen area?

Yes — lanai and pool-deck tile is a signature Lakewood Ranch project for us. Outdoor work changes the spec: porcelain rated for exterior use with a wet-area slip rating, uncoupling membranes to absorb the slab's thermal movement, exterior-grade polymer grout, and expansion joints laid out for surfaces that swing forty degrees between a July afternoon and a January night. Done to that standard, the lanai floor outlasts the screen cage around it.

Do you build curbless showers for master bathrooms in Lakewood Ranch?

We do, and demand keeps growing — both for the spa look in Country Club East remodels and for practical accessibility in Del Webb and Cresswind. A curbless shower is a drainage engineering exercise: the slab gets recessed or the floor built up so the shower plane slopes to a linear drain while staying flush with the bathroom floor, then the whole zone is waterproofed as one continuous membrane before a single tile is set.

How do you prevent lippage on big 24x48 porcelain tiles?

Four controls, applied together: substrate flattened to the large-format standard before setting; a directional back-butter and trowel technique that achieves full mortar coverage; a leveling-clip system that mechanically locks adjacent edges flush while the mortar cures; and offset limits — never stacking joints at 50% on long tiles, where manufacturer warpage concentrates. Lippage is a system failure, not bad luck, and each of those steps has its own checkpoint in our protocol.

Does interior tile work in Lakewood Ranch need HOA or design-review approval?

Interior floor tile itself rarely requires approval, but adjacent details often do: some estate villages review exterior-visible lanai changes, and attached villas in several Ranch communities regulate hard flooring on upper levels for sound. If your project touches a lanai, an upstairs floor or a shared wall, we check your village's documents during the estimate and fold any required paperwork into the schedule so nothing stalls at tile-setting week.

How long does a master bathroom tile renovation take in Lakewood Ranch?

A full master bath — demo, shower rebuild, floor and wall tile, grout and sealing — typically runs two to three weeks, with waterproofing cure times and inspection holds accounting for several of those days. We stage the work so your home's second bathroom carries the load, protect the path from the front door daily, and give you a written schedule up front so the whirlpool tub isn't sitting in the garage a day longer than planned.

Material 101

Porcelain vs. ceramic: same family, different animals

Both are clay tiles fired in a kiln — the difference is the recipe and the temperature. Porcelain uses finer clays fired hotter, producing a tile so dense it absorbs less than half a percent of water; that density is why it survives pool decks, lanais and commercial traffic. Ceramic is fired softer, cuts easier, weighs less and costs less, which keeps it the sensible pick for backsplashes, shower walls and guest-bath floors that see slippers more than sand. A quick rule for Manatee County homes: porcelain underfoot, ceramic on the walls — then break the rule deliberately when budget or a specific look justifies it. Through-body and color-body porcelains hide edge chips better than glazed ceramic, another quiet advantage in sandy, high-traffic entries.

The Florida Favorite

Wood-look tile: why half our tile jobs are planks

Walk any model home from Heritage Harbour to Lakewood Ranch and you'll find porcelain planks pretending to be white oak. The appeal is simple: Floridians want wood warmth without wood worry. A plank tile floor ignores the humidity that moves real wood, laughs at wet swimsuits and dog bowls, and never needs sanding — while modern inkjet glazing varies the grain so convincingly that repeats are hard to spot across a great room.

The catch is that plank tile is the most demanding format we set. Long tiles bow slightly in firing, so an installer who stacks them half-offset like real hardwood creates ridges at every joint. Our crews cap offsets at one-third of plank length, upgrade to tighter flatness prep, and run leveling clips on every row — which is why our wood-look floors read flat in raking evening light, the harshest test a tile floor faces.

Deciding between plank tile and vinyl plank? The honest breakdown lives in our LVP vs. tile in Florida guide, and the LVP page makes the case for the other side.

Specialty Work

Large format, showers, backsplashes and repairs

📏 Large-format & lippage control

Tiles 24x24 and up deliver seamless, modern floors — if the slab beneath them is flattened to the tighter tolerance the format demands. We map flatness first, grind and fill to spec, verify mortar coverage by lifting test tiles, and clip every edge until cure. The result is a floor your bare feet can't find a ridge on.

🚿 Showers & wet areas

Every shower we build gets a bonded waterproofing membrane over the pan and walls, sloped correctly to the drain and flood-tested for 24 hours before tile is set — the standard Florida code inspectors want to see and the step failed showers always skipped. Niches, benches and linear drains are built in, not bolted on.

🦶 Curbless & aging-in-place

Zero-entry showers, lever-reachable niches and slip-rated tile (we spec DCOF ratings, not guesses) make bathrooms safer without looking clinical. For Bradenton's 55+ communities this is now our most requested bathroom configuration, often paired with grab-bar blocking hidden behind the tile.

🍳 Kitchen backsplashes

Subway, zellige, mosaic sheet or slab porcelain — backsplashes are precision trim work with outlets, windows and uneven drywall to negotiate. We laser-level the first course, miter outside corners and caulk change-of-plane joints so the finish looks intentional up close, where backsplashes are actually viewed.

🔧 Repair & regrouting

Sound tile with tired joints doesn't need replacement. We grind out failed grout, address hollow-sounding or cracked tiles individually, regrout with stain-resistant formulas and re-caulk movement joints that should never have been grouted. A two-day refresh routinely buys a 20-year-old floor another decade.

⛏ Old tile removal done right

Demolition is loud, dusty and half the job. Our tear-outs include dust-controlled grinding of the leftover thin-set — because new tile bonded to old ridges fails early, and a "cleared" slab is not the same as a flat one.

Protocol Deep Dive

Installation & Transitions: the 12 checkpoints that make tile flat

Tile is unforgiving — mistakes cure permanently into mortar. Within The 47-Point Coastal Subfloor & Install Protocol, the Installation & Transitions category carries 12 checkpoints, and tile jobs exercise every one of them:

Checkpoints 1–4 · Set-upBefore mortar opens
  • 1. Layout centered on sightlines, approved in a dry-lay
  • 2. Correct trowel notch selected; back-butter on large format
  • 3. Offset capped at one-third for plank formats
  • 4. Door jambs and casings undercut for slid-under edges
Checkpoints 5–8 · SettingWhile it cures
  • 5. Mortar coverage verified by periodic lift checks
  • 6. Leveling clips on every tile edge 15" or longer
  • 7. Movement joints honored at rooms, sun zones and perimeters
  • 8. Wet-area membranes flood-tested before any tile is set
Checkpoints 9–12 · FinishingThe details you touch
  • 9. Doorway transitions cut and fitted, never smeared with caulk
  • 10. Stair nosings and edge profiles fastened to spec
  • 11. Grout joints gauged for consistency; plane changes caulked, not grouted
  • 12. Baseboards reinstalled, caulked and paint-ready

The other four categories — Slab Moisture & Flatness (11), Acoustic & HOA Compliance (8), Material Acclimation & Layout (9) and Final Walkthrough & Care Plan (7) — round out the 47 checkpoints documented on your signed completion sheet.

Tile Questions

Tile installation FAQs

Porcelain or ceramic tile — which should I choose?

Porcelain is fired denser and absorbs less than 0.5% water, so it wears harder, resists staining better and can be rated for outdoor and lanai use; it costs more and demands more skill to cut and set. Ceramic is softer, lighter, easier to work and friendlier to the budget, which makes it a fine choice for walls, backsplashes and lighter-traffic floors. For Gulf Coast floors that see sand, pets and pool traffic, we usually recommend porcelain and reserve ceramic for vertical surfaces.

What is wood-look tile and does it feel like real wood?

Wood-look tile is porcelain plank — typically 6x36 or 8x48 inches — printed with high-resolution wood grain and often textured to match. Underfoot it feels like tile: harder and cooler than wood, which many Floridians count as a feature in summer. What it gives you is a wood living room that can take a flooded washer, a wet dog and full sun without cupping, fading or refinishing, at $8–$16 per square foot installed in the 2026 market.

How do you keep large-format tile flat and lippage-free?

Four disciplines: the slab is flattened to the tighter tolerance large tile demands, planks are offset no more than a third of their length so bowed centers never meet edges, mortar coverage is verified by lifting tiles during the set, and clip-and-wedge leveling systems hold neighboring edges flush while the thin-set cures. Skipping any one of them is how you get the shadowed, toe-catching edges you can spot in hallways all over town.

Can you build a curbless shower in an existing Bradenton bathroom?

Usually yes, even on a slab. We recess the shower zone by grinding the slab or raising the surrounding floor, set a linear drain, then waterproof the entire wet area with a bonded membrane before tiling. Curbless entries are our most-requested aging-in-place upgrade in 55+ communities, and they must be flood-tested before tile goes on — a step our protocol makes non-negotiable.

Do you repair and regrout existing tile without replacing it?

Yes. If your tile is sound but the joints are stained, cracked or crumbling, we remove the old grout mechanically, treat any hairline cracks or hollow tiles, and regrout with modern stain-resistant grout — typically $10–$25 per linear foot as a 2026 market range, or quoted by room. We also replace individual cracked tiles when attic stock or a close match exists, and re-caulk tub and shower joints that were grouted when they should have been flexible.

How soon can I walk on newly installed tile?

Light foot traffic is fine after about 24 hours with standard thin-set, and furniture can return after grout cures — usually 48 to 72 hours. Rapid-set mortars can shorten that to same-evening walking when a bathroom can't be out of service long. Showers need longer: we hold new wet areas out of use until grout and sealant have fully cured, and your care sheet lists the exact dates for your products.

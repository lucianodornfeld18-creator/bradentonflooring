Parrish Questions

New-construction LVP questions we answer daily in 34219

My Parrish house is barely a year old — why does the slab still need moisture testing?

Because concrete keeps releasing water vapor long after it looks and feels dry. A slab poured six months before your closing in North River Ranch or Summerwoods can still read well above the vapor-emission limits plank manufacturers set for their warranties. Skipping the test and getting lucky happens; skipping it and getting cupped planks, adhesive failure or mold under a floating floor also happens. A thirty-dollar test protects a ten-thousand-dollar floor, so we never skip it.

Should I swap the builder carpet right after closing, or wait a few years?

If you already know you want hard flooring in the bedrooms, doing it before move-in is dramatically easier: no furniture to stage, no living around the work, and the carpet is worth nothing more used than new. The one caution is slab readiness — on very fresh Silverleaf and Copperstone builds we sometimes recommend a vapor-barrier underlayment upgrade or a short wait based on the actual RH reading, and we tell you which honestly.

Will replacing my floors void the builder warranty on a new Parrish home?

Replacing flooring does not void a structural or systems warranty, but the builder will no longer cover the flooring itself or claim responsibility for slab-related floor issues they didn't install over. That is precisely why our documentation matters: dated moisture readings and flatness maps from before our install prove the slab's condition, which protects you in any later dispute about whether a problem originated with the concrete or the covering.

How long should a new concrete slab cure before LVP goes down?

The honest answer is that the calendar matters less than the measurement. Industry rules of thumb say 60 to 90 days minimum, but Florida's water table and rainy-season pours make some Parrish slabs test wet at six months while others pass at ninety days. We qualify slabs by in-situ relative humidity probes, not guesswork — and when a reading is marginal, a moisture-rated underlayment or epoxy mitigation lets the project proceed safely instead of waiting indefinitely.

What's actually different between the builder's LVP and what you install?

Almost everything except the name. Production builders in the 34219 corridor typically install a 6-to-12-mil wear layer, 4-to-5-inch plank with a paper-thin attached pad, chosen because it prices well across four hundred houses. The mid and premium products we carry double or triple the wear layer, widen the plank, deepen the embossing and add a real acoustic pad — which is why one floor shows traffic lanes in three years and the other doesn't.

Can you finish the install between my closing date and move-in day?

That window is our specialty in Parrish. Give us your closing date a few weeks ahead and we pre-order material, run slab tests the day after keys, and typically complete a full single-story swap-out in three to five days — done before the moving truck arrives. Empty-house installs also price better because there's no furniture handling or room-by-room sequencing to slow the crew down.
